About the summit

What is the summit

The 2026 BIPOC Mental Health Summit, hosted by the Oregon Heals Coalition, is a one-day convening featuring workshops, speakers, and community activations — a space for collective healing, learning, and community transformation.

Our theme, Healing Together: Across Generations, Across Communities, is grounded in the belief that mental health extends beyond the individual. The summit seeks to shift the narrative from viewing mental health solely as a personal experience to understanding it as a reflection of the health and well-being of entire communities.

Audience

Who it's for

Join community members, youth, and mental health professionals for programming centered on meaningful dialogue, shared wisdom, and collective action, as we envision a more holistic system of mental health care. Together, we seek to break cycles of trauma, dismantle systemic barriers to care, and build a future where healing is accessible, culturally grounded, and collective.

Continuing education credits. The summit will provide certificates of attendance that can be used to obtain CEUs.

Why now

Why this matters

The current mental health system does not adequately serve all people, particularly Black, Indigenous, and People of Color. Equitable access to affordable, culturally responsive, and community-informed care is essential to addressing longstanding disparities and healing the impacts of intergenerational trauma.

Healing is not one-size-fits-all. It can take many forms, including reclaiming forgotten cultural healing practices, fostering multigenerational conversations, and building community-centered pathways to wellness.

Community Healing Panel: Community Care & Safety as Praxis to Destigmatizing Mental Health St. Helens Ballroom

Moderator: Yeda Song, Peace by Piece

Panelists: Martin Lockett, MS, CADC II · Melody Li, LMFT · Spencer Uemura · CéShaun Hankins · Judith Faustima

What does it take to make mental health care feel safe, accessible, and relevant to the communities it serves? Mental health does not exist in isolation or solely within the walls of a therapist's office. Panelists will discuss approaches to prevention and crisis response, building community capacity to recognize and respond to mental health concerns, and creating safer care pathways for people of color who have historically faced barriers, discrimination, or mistrust within health systems. Together, we will examine how safety, connection, cultural responsiveness, and community empowerment can move mental health care from a reactive service to care models that honor culture, identity, lived experience, and the unique needs of their communities.

Youth & Young Adult Mental Health through Sama Samang Pagkilos (Collective Action) Garden

Facilitator: Ira Mae Nicolas, Kaya! Kabataan! (Filipino Bayanihan Center)

What are the impacts of our surroundings on our mental health? How can coming together to create change build confidence and leadership? This workshop will showcase the work of youth from the Kaya! Kabataan! youth program and local community organizers to change the name of Colonel Summers Park. We will also screen a mini-documentary in progress, expressing the strength of pro-people education and action.

Professional Development Sustaining the Frontline: Supporting Suicide Prevention Through Equity Cascade

Facilitator: Roberto Osorio, Oregon Health Authority — Behavioral Health Division, Policy Analyst

Youth and adults in Oregon experience clear disparities in suicide deaths, especially in communities who face systemic barriers and historic marginalization. Culturally specific providers are often the first people these communities turn to for support — they bring lived experience, cultural knowledge, and trusted relationships, but they also carry heavy workload demands, chronic exposure to system-level harm, and burnout. This session shares how the Behavioral Health Equity & Community Partnerships team is shifting how we work with culturally specific partners: practicing radical listening and space holding, showing up without a preset agenda, learning directly from providers, and centering the needs of communities instead of system requirements.

11:45 AM – 1:00 PM

Special session

Reclaiming the Power of Storytelling through Poetry

Christopher Diaz

St. Helens Ballroom

About the workshop. What stories shape our sense of self, and what leads us to believe in those stories? How can poetry support our mental health, especially for those intimidated by the genre? In this workshop, we'll explore how the expansive nature and permissions of poetry can help us connect with ourselves, each other, and the world around us. From the personal to the political, we'll engage with poems and have a chance to create some, too. Participants can expect to leave with tips and exercises for accessing the power of poetry, with the gentle reminder that this storytelling tool belongs to all of us.

About Christopher. Christopher Sablan Diaz is a poet, performer, and indigenous CHamoru from the Pacific Island of Guåhan. He's been featured by NBC News and has received support from Indigenous Nations Poets, Kundiman, the McCormack Writing Center, the Washington State Arts Commission, and the Sitka Center for Art & Ecology. His poems have been interrogating masculinity, deconstructing his military service, and exploring the grief of assimilation and intergenerational trauma. He's taught poetry with "Writers in the Schools" and co-facilitates monthly writing workshops at the juvenile detention center in Vancouver, WA.

Concurrent Breakout Sessions #2

Community Healing Sanando Collective Trauma through Our Activismo & Cultura St. Helens Ballroom

Facilitator: Faviola Robles Saenz, PhD, Community Psychologist, Seguir Adelante Research Group

Join us to explore the history and research behind migration, anti-immigrant sentiment, and collective trauma experienced by Latino immigrants. Together, we will also dive into the rich history of our activismo and cultura, learning how we can lean into these roots to find strength, resistance, and healing.

Youth & Young Adult We Are Mental Health: Impact of Youth Peer-to-Peer Training in BIPOC Communities Garden

Facilitators: Devin Thongdy · Ron Sagun · Minh Nguyen (YouthLine)

Join Devin, Ron, and Minh from YouthLine as they present on the peer-to-peer crisis hotline, topics in BIPOC Youth Mental Health, and the importance of peer support in the healing process within BIPOC communities. The session opens into a Q&A with youth panelists Xutong Hei, Keem Gian, Ron Sagun, and Minh Nguyen, moderated by Devin Thongdy. YouthLine is a peer-to-peer crisis hotline dedicated to reaching folks through text, chat, and call — volunteers are aged 15–24.

Professional Development Holding Both: Clinical Practice for BIPOC Therapists Working With White Clients in Portland, Oregon Cascade

Facilitator: Marie Mellberg, LPC — Clinical Rehabilitation Counselor, Licensed Professional Counselor, Counselor Educator, and Clinical Supervisor

This workshop helps participants identify common clinical dynamics such as cultural taxation, racialized transference and countertransference, and expectations of racial labor when working with white clients. It emphasizes ethical, sustainable practice strategies, including boundary-setting and consultation practices aligned with the ACA Code of Ethics. The workshop also incorporates creative arts techniques to explore the relationship between personal/cultural identity and professional clinical identity — promoting reflection, community building, and self-care among BIPOC clinicians working in predominantly white settings.

3:30 – 4:30 PM

Keynote

Councilor Tiffany Koyama Lane

St. Helens Ballroom

Councilor Koyama Lane, also known as Teacher Tiffany, is an award-winning educator with 16 years of experience teaching in public schools. A proud leader in her union, she has spent years fighting for vibrant, safe classrooms for Portland students, and better conditions for public school educators and staff. As a fourth-generation Japanese American (Yonsei), Councilor Koyama Lane stands on the shoulders of generations of people working for change. She lives in Southeast Portland with her husband and two young kids.

Highlights

  • First Asian American woman elected to Portland City Council, with Japanese American grandparents who were incarcerated during WWII.
  • Unanimously elected the first Council Vice President under Portland's new form of government; had a key role in building the Council's processes, rules, and shared-leadership culture from scratch.
  • Builds countless relationships in the community and supports community groups, small businesses, and individuals.
  • Currently holds the Vice Chair position of the Finance & Governance Committee of the Whole for oversight, transparency, accountability, and community involvement.
  • Currently sits on the Housing & Permitting and Public Works committees.
